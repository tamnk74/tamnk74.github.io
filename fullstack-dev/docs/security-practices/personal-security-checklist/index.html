Authentication​

Most reported data breaches are caused by the use of weak, default, or stolen passwords (according to this Verizon report). Use long, strong, and unique passwords, manage them in a secure password manager, enable 2-factor authentication, keep on top of breaches, and take care while logging into your accounts.

SecurityPriorityDetails and Hints
Use a Strong PasswordEssentialIf your password is too short, or contains dictionary words, places, or names, then it can be easily cracked through brute force or guessed by someone. The easiest way to make a strong password is by making it long (12+ characters) — consider using a 'passphrase' made up of many words. Alternatively, use a password generator to create a long, strong random password. Have a play with Security.org's How Secure Is My Password?, to get an idea of how quickly common passwords can be cracked. Read more about creating strong passwords: securityinabox.org.
Don't Reuse PasswordsEssentialIf someone were to reuse a password and one site they had an account with suffered a leak, then a criminal could easily gain unauthorized access to their other accounts. This is usually done through large-scale automated login requests, and it is called Credential Stuffing. Unfortunately, this is all too common, but it's simple to protect against — use a different password for each of your online accounts.
Use a Secure Password ManagerEssentialFor most people, it is going to be near-impossible to remember hundreds of strong and unique passwords. A password manager is an application that generates, stores, and auto-fills your login credentials for you. All your passwords will be encrypted against 1 master password (which you must remember, and it should be very strong). Most password managers have browser extensions and mobile apps, so whatever device you are on, your passwords can be auto-filled. A good all-rounder is Bitwarden, or see Recommended Password Managers.
Avoid Sharing PasswordsEssentialWhile there may be times that you need to share access to an account with another person, you should generally avoid doing this because it makes it easier for the account to become compromised. If you absolutely do need to share a password — for example, when working on a team with a shared account — this should be done via features built into a password manager.
Enable 2-Factor AuthenticationEssential2FA is where you must provide both something you know (a password) and something you have (such as a code on your phone) to log in. This means that if anyone has your password (e.g., through phishing, malware, or a data breach), they will not be able to log into your account. It's easy to get started, download an authenticator app onto your phone, and then go to your account security settings and follow the steps to enable 2FA. Next time you log in on a new device, you will be prompted for the code that is displayed in the app on your phone (it works without internet, and the code usually changes every 30 seconds).
Keep Backup Codes SafeEssentialWhen you enable multi-factor authentication, you will usually be given several codes that you can use if your 2FA method is lost, broken, or unavailable. Keep these codes somewhere safe to prevent loss or unauthorized access. You should store these on paper or in a safe place on disk (e.g., in offline storage or an encrypted file/drive). Don't store these in your password manager as 2FA sources and passwords should be kept separately.
Sign Up for Breach AlertsOptionalAfter a website suffers a significant data breach, the leaked data often ends up on the internet. Several websites collect these leaked records and allow you to search your email address to check if you are in any of their lists. Firefox Monitor, Have I Been Pwned, and DeHashed allow you to sign up for monitoring, where they will notify you if your email address appears in any new data sets.
Shield your Password/PINOptionalWhen typing your password in public places, ensure you are not in direct line of sight of a CCTV camera and that no one can see over your shoulder. Cover your password or pin code while you type, and do not reveal any plain text passwords on your screen.
Update Critical Passwords PeriodicallyOptionalDatabase leaks and breaches are common, and, likely, several of your passwords are already somewhere online. Occasionally updating passwords of security-critical accounts can help mitigate this. But providing that all your passwords are long, strong, and unique, there is no need to do this too often — annually should be sufficient.
Don't Save your Password in BrowsersOptionalMost modern browsers offer to save your credentials when you log into a site. Don't allow this, as they are not always encrypted and could allow someone to gain access to your accounts. Instead, use a dedicated password manager to store (and auto-fill) your passwords.
Avoid Logging In on Someone Else's DeviceOptionalAvoid logging in on other people's computers since you can't be sure their system is clean. Be especially cautious of public machines, as malware and tracking are more common here. When using someone else's machine, ensure that you're in a private/incognito session (Use Ctrl+Shift+N/ Cmd+Shift+N).
Avoid Password HintsOptionalSome sites allow you to set password hints. Often, it is very easy to guess answers. In cases where password hints are mandatory, use random answers and record them in your password manager.
Never Answer Online Security Questions TruthfullyOptionalIf a site asks security questions (such as place of birth, mother's maiden name, or first car, etc.), don't provide real answers. Instead, create a fictitious answer, and store it inside your password manager.
Don't Use a 4-digit PINOptionalDon't use a short PIN to access your smartphone or computer. Instead, use a text password or a much longer PIN. Numeric passphrases are easy to crack (A 4-digit pin has 10,000 combinations, compared to 7.4 million for a 4-character alpha-numeric code).
Avoid Using SMS for 2FAOptionalWhen enabling multi-factor authentication, opt for app-based codes or a hardware token if supported. SMS is susceptible to several common threats, such as SIM-swapping and interception.
Avoid Using your PM to Generate OTPsAdvancedMany password managers are also able to generate 2FA codes. It is best not to use your primary password manager as your 2FA authenticator as well, since it would become a single point of failure if compromised. Instead, use a dedicated authenticator app on your phone or laptop.
Avoid Face UnlockAdvancedMost phones and laptops offer a facial recognition authentication feature. It may be very convenient, but there are numerous ways to fool it and gain access to the device.
Watch Out for KeyloggersAdvancedA hardware keylogger is a physical device planted between your keyboard and the USB port, which intercepts all keystrokes. The best way to stay protected is just by checking your USB connection after your PC has been unattended.
Consider a Hardware TokenAdvancedA U2F/FIDO2 security key is a USB (or NFC) device that you insert while logging in to an online service to verify your identity. SoloKey and NitroKey are examples of such keys.
Consider Offline Password ManagerAdvancedFor increased security, an encrypted offline password manager will give you full control over your data. KeePass is a popular choice.
Consider Unique UsernamesAdvancedHaving different passwords for each account is a good first step, but if you also use a unique username, email, or phone number to log in, then it will be significantly harder for anyone trying to gain unauthorised access.

Web Browsing​

Most websites on the internet will use some form of tracking, often to gain insight into their users behaviour and preferences. This section outlines the steps you can take, to be better protected from threats, minimise online tracking and improve privacy.

SecurityPriorityDetails and Hints
Block AdsEssentialUsing an ad-blocker can help improve your privacy, by blocking the trackers that ads implement. uBlock Origin is a very efficient and open source browser addon.
Ensure Website is LegitimateBasicWhen logging into any online accounts, double check the URL is correct. You can check a website using tools like Virus Total, IsLegitSite.
Watch out for Browser MalwareBasicCommon signs include: default search engine or homepage has been modified, unfamiliar extensions, significantly more ads, errors and slow loading.
Use a Privacy-Respecting BrowserEssentialFirefox (with tweaks) and Brave are secure, private-respecting browsers. Avoid Chrome, Edge and Safari without proper configuration.
Use a Private Search EngineEssentialConsider DuckDuckGo, or Qwant. Google implements invasive tracking policies.
Remove Unnecessary Browser AddonsEssentialExtensions can see, log or modify anything you do in the browser. Only install extensions you really need.
Keep Browser Up-to-dateEssentialBrowser vulnerabilities are constantly being discovered and patched, so keep it updated.
Check for HTTPSEssentialDo not enter any data on a non-HTTPS website. Most modern browsers now have HTTPS-only mode built in.
Use DNS-over-HTTPSEssentialDNS-over-HTTPS performs DNS resolution via the HTTPS protocol, meaning data between you and your DNS resolver is encrypted.
Multi-Session ContainersEssentialUse Firefox Containers or different browsers for different tasks.
Use IncognitoEssentialWhen using someone else's machine, use private/incognito mode to prevent browser history and cookies from being saved.
Understand Your Browser FingerprintEssentialVisit amiunique.org to view your fingerprint. The aim is to be as un-unique as possible.
Manage CookiesEssentialClear cookies regularly to reduce tracking.
Block Third-Party CookiesEssentialThird-party cookies pose privacy risks. Disable them in browser settings.
Block Third-Party TrackersEssentialUse Privacy Badger, DuckDuckGo Privacy Essentials, or uBlock Origin.
Beware of RedirectsOptionalCheck redirect URLs with tools like RedirectDetective if unsure.
Do Not Sign Into Your BrowserOptionalSigning into browsers allows for data collection and increases attack surface.
Disallow Prediction ServicesOptionalPrediction services send data to search engines with every keypress. Disable them.
Avoid G Translate for WebpagesOptionalGoogle collects all data when translating. Use alternative translation services.
Disable Web NotificationsOptionalBrowser push notifications can be spoofed. Disable them for security.
Disable Automatic DownloadsOptionalDisable auto file downloads to prevent drive-by downloads.
Disallow Access to SensorsOptionalMobile websites can access device sensors. Disable these permissions.
Disallow LocationOptionalDisable location services in browser settings.
Disallow Camera/Microphone accessOptionalEnsure no websites have camera or microphone access.
Disable Browser Password SavesOptionalUse a password manager instead of browser password storage.
Disable Browser AutofillOptionalTurn off autofill for confidential information.
Protect from Exfil AttackOptionalUse CSS Exfil Protection plugin.
Deactivate ActiveXOptionalActiveX is dangerous and should be disabled (mainly for older IE browsers).
Disable WebRTCOptionalWebRTC can leak your IP address even when using a VPN.
Spoof HTML5 Canvas SigOptionalUse Canvas-Fingerprint-Blocker extension or Tor to prevent canvas fingerprinting.
Spoof User AgentOptionalPeriodically switch user agents to become less unique.
Disregard DNTOptionalDo Not Track has limited impact and may make you more unique.
Prevent HSTS TrackingOptionalDisable via chrome://net-internals/#hsts in Chromium browsers.
Prevent Automatic Browser ConnectionsOptionalDisable analytics and diagnostics reporting in browser settings.
Enable 1st-Party IsolationOptionalFirst Party Isolation reduces tracking significantly.
Strip Tracking Params from URLsAdvancedUse ClearURLs to automatically strip tracking data.
First Launch SecurityAdvancedConfigure privacy settings before first connecting to the internet after browser installation.
Use The Tor BrowserAdvancedTor browser encrypts and routes traffic through multiple nodes for privacy.
Disable JavaScriptAdvancedDisabling JavaScript reduces attack surface but significantly impacts browsing experience.

Email​

Email-related fraud is on the rise, and without taking basic measures you could be at risk. If a hacker gets access to your emails, it provides a gateway for your other accounts to be compromised through password resets.

SecurityPriorityDetails and Hints
Have more than one email addressEssentialUse different email addresses for security-critical communications versus newsletters to compartmentalize data.
Keep Email Address PrivateEssentialDon't share your primary email publicly as addresses are starting points for phishing attacks.
Keep your Account SecureEssentialUse a long, unique password, enable 2FA, and be careful while logging in.
Disable Automatic Loading of Remote ContentEssentialDisable automatic loading of images/stylesheets as it exposes your IP and is used for tracking.
Use PlaintextOptionalPlaintext emails are preferred over HTML for privacy and security. See UsePlaintext.email.
Don't connect third-party appsOptionalThird-party apps with inbox access pose significant security and privacy risks.
Don't Share Sensitive Data via EmailOptionalEmails are easily intercepted and cannot be considered safe unless encrypted.
Consider Switching to Secure Mail ProviderOptionalUse providers like Forward Email, ProtonMail, or Tutanota.
Use Smart KeyAdvancedUse USB Smart Keys for signing/decrypting without exposing private keys.
Use Aliasing / Anonymous ForwardingAdvancedEmail aliasing allows unique addresses per service to track data leaks.
SubaddressingOptionalUse the + symbol for subaddressing (e.g., email+service@domain.com).
Use a Custom DomainAdvancedCustom domains prevent dependency on specific mail providers.
Sync with a client for backupAdvancedUse Thunderbird to backup emails locally via IMAP.
Be Careful with Mail SignaturesAdvancedEmail signatures can leak contact information through automated crawlers.
Be Careful with Auto-RepliesAdvancedDon't reveal too much in out-of-office replies.
Choose the Right Mail ProtocolAdvancedUse IMAPv4 or POPv3 minimum; avoid outdated protocols.
Self-HostingAdvancedNot recommended for non-advanced users due to security complexity.
Always use TLS PortsAdvancedUse SSL ports for POP3, IMAP, and SMTP instead of plaintext.
DNS AvailabilityAdvancedUse multiple MX records for redundancy in self-hosted setups.
Prevent DDoS and Brute ForceAdvancedLimit simultaneous connections for self-hosted SMTP servers.
Maintain IP BlacklistAdvancedKeep updated IP blacklists for spam filtering on self-hosted servers.

Messaging​

Secure messaging is essential for private communications. This section covers best practices for messaging apps.

SecurityPriorityDetails and Hints
Only Use Fully End-to-End Encrypted MessengersEssentialE2E encryption ensures messages are encrypted on your device and only decrypted by the recipient.
Use only Open Source Messaging PlatformsEssentialOpen source code can be independently audited for security issues.
Use a "Trustworthy" Messaging PlatformEssentialEnsure apps are fully open source, stable, actively maintained, and backed by reputable developers.
Check Security SettingsEssentialEnable contact verification, security notifications, and encryption. Disable read receipts and typing notifications.
Ensure your Recipients Environment is SecureEssentialYour conversation is only as secure as the weakest link.
Disable Cloud ServicesEssentialAvoid web/desktop companions as they increase attack surface.
Secure Group ChatsEssentialRisk increases with more participants. Periodically verify all participants.
Create a Safe EnvironmentEssentialBe aware of monitoring points: devices, ISP, national gateways, messaging providers, servers.
Agree on a Communication PlanOptionalHave primary and backup secure communication methods.
Strip Meta-Data from MediaOptionalRemove metadata from photos, videos, documents before sending.
Defang URLsOptionalBe cautious with link previews that may leak information.
Verify your RecipientOptionalUse apps with contact verification features.
Enable Ephemeral MessagesOptionalSelf-destructing messages limit exposure if device is compromised.
Avoid SMSOptionalSMS is not secure and susceptible to interception and sim swapping.
Watch out for TrackersOptionalBe wary of messaging apps with invasive trackers.
Consider JurisdictionAdvancedConsider where the organization is based and data is hosted.
Use an Anonymous PlatformAdvancedUse platforms not requiring phone numbers for maximum anonymity.
Ensure Forward SecrecyAdvancedChoose platforms implementing forward secrecy with new keys per message.
Consider a Decentralized PlatformAdvancedDecentralized platforms avoid single point of trust.

Social Media​

Social networking sites collect extensive data about individuals and sell it to third parties. Secure your account and understand that uploaded data is effectively public.

SecurityPriorityDetails and Hints
Secure your AccountEssentialUse unique, strong passwords and enable 2-factor authentication.
Check Privacy SettingsEssentialEnsure you're comfortable with what data you're exposing and to whom.
Think of All Interactions as PublicEssentialPrivate content can still be viewed through various methods.
Think of All Interactions as PermanentEssentialContent is continuously archived by third-party services.
Don't Reveal too MuchEssentialAvoid sharing DoB, hometown, school etc. which helps hackers personalize attacks.
Be Careful what you UploadEssentialPosts, photos, videos can reveal more than intended.
Don't Share Email or Phone NumberEssentialProtects against hackers, trolls, spammers, and data connection.
Don't Grant Unnecessary PermissionsEssentialOnly grant permissions that apps actually need.
Be Careful of 3rd-Party IntegrationsEssentialAvoid social network logins; revoke unused app access.
Avoid Publishing Geo Data while OnsiteEssentialWait until you've left a location before sharing it.
Remove metadata before uploadingOptionalStrip EXIF data from photos before uploading.
Implement Image CloakingAdvancedUse tools like Fawkes to prevent facial recognition.
Consider Spoofing GPSAdvancedOthers' posts can reveal your location even if you don't use social media.
Consider False InformationAdvancedUse alias names and false details if just reading.
Don't have social media accountsAdvancedFor maximum privacy, avoid mainstream social networks entirely.

Networks​

This section covers securing your internet connection, including router configuration and VPN setup.

SecurityPriorityDetails and Hints
Use a VPNEssentialUse a reputable, paid VPN to protect your IP, reduce ISP data collection, and increase public WiFi protection.
Change your Router PasswordEssentialChange default router passwords as they're publicly available.
Use WPA2/WPA3EssentialUse WPA2 or WPA3 authentication protocols with strong passwords.
Keep router firmware up-to-dateEssentialUpdates fix vulnerabilities and improve performance.
Implement Network-Wide VPNOptionalConfigure VPN on router/firewall for all device traffic encryption.
Protect against DNS leaksOptionalUse only your VPN provider's DNS or secure DNS service.
Use a secure VPN ProtocolOptionalUse OpenVPN or WireGuard; avoid PPTP or SSTP.
Secure DNSOptionalUse DNS-over-HTTPS for encrypted DNS resolution.
Avoid ISP's free routerOptionalThey often have insecure firmware without regular updates.
Whitelist MAC AddressesOptionalOnly allow known devices to connect to your network.
Change Router's Local IPOptionalPrevents exploitation of cross-site scripting vulnerabilities.
Don't Reveal Personal Info in SSIDOptionalChoose SSID that doesn't identify you or device brands.
Opt-Out Router ListingsOptionalRemove your SSID from public WiFi databases.
Hide your SSIDOptionalInvisible SSIDs receive less abuse.
Disable WPSOptionalWPS has major security vulnerabilities.
Disable UPnPOptionalUPnP has a history of security issues.
Use Guest NetworkOptionalDon't grant visitors access to primary network.
Change Router's Default IPOptionalMakes malicious script targeting harder.
Kill unused servicesOptionalDisable Telnet, SSH unless actually needed.
Don't have Open PortsOptionalClose unnecessary open ports.
Disable Unused Remote ProtocolsOptionalDisable PING, Telnet, SSH, UPnP, HNAP when not needed.
Disable Cloud ManagementOptionalProtect router admin panel from cloud-based access.
Manage Range CorrectlyOptionalDon't extend WiFi range beyond what's needed.
Route through TorAdvancedRoute all traffic through Tor network for increased security.
Disable WiFiAdvancedUse Ethernet connections only to minimize attack surface.

Mobile Devices​

Smartphones track location, can eavesdrop via microphones, watch through cameras, and generate extensive data about users. This section covers mobile security best practices.

SecurityPriorityDetails and Hints
Encrypt your DeviceEssentialUse file encryption to protect data from physical access.
Turn off unused connectivityEssentialDisable WiFi, Bluetooth, NFC when not in use.
Minimize app countEssentialUninstall unused apps that run in background.
App PermissionsEssentialOnly grant necessary permissions. Use Bouncer for temporary permissions.
Install from official sources onlyEssentialUse App Store or Play Store for scanned, verified apps.
Be Careful of Juice JackingOptionalAvoid public charging stations or use USB data blockers.
Set up mobile carrier PINEssentialProtect against SIM hijacking with carrier PIN.
Opt-out of Caller ID ListingsOptionalUnlist from apps like TrueCaller, CallApp.
Use Offline MapsOptionalUse OsmAnd or Organic Maps to reduce data leaks.
Opt-out of personalized adsOptionalReduce data collection through ad settings.
Erase after login attemptsOptionalAuto-erase after too many failed pins.
Monitor TrackersOptionalUse εxodus to check app trackers.
Use Mobile FirewallOptionalInstall firewall app to prevent data leaks.
Reduce Background ActivityOptionalUse SuperFreeze to freeze background app activities.
Sandbox Mobile AppsOptionalUse Island for app sandboxing.
Tor TrafficAdvancedUse Orbot for system-wide Tor connection.
Avoid Custom KeyboardsOptionalStick with stock keyboard or use reputable alternatives.
Restart RegularlyOptionalRestart phone weekly to clear cached app states.
Avoid SMSOptionalUse encrypted messaging like Signal instead.
Keep Number PrivateOptionalUse virtual numbers via MySudo.
Watch for StalkerwareOptionalFactory reset to remove stalkerware.
Favor Browser over AppsOptionalUse secure browser instead of dedicated apps where possible.
Consider Custom ROMAdvancedUse privacy-focused custom ROMs on Android.

Personal Computers​

Your OS provides the interface between hardware and applications, so if compromised can have detrimental effects.

SecurityPriorityDetails and Hints
Keep System UpdatedEssentialInstall updates promptly for security fixes.
Encrypt your DeviceEssentialUse BitLocker (Windows), FileVault (macOS), or LUKS (Linux) for full disk encryption.
Backup Important DataEssentialUse Cryptomator or VeraCrypt for encrypted backups.
Be Careful with USB DevicesEssentialUSB devices can pose serious threats.
Activate Screen-LockEssentialLock when away; require password on resume.
Disable Voice AssistantsEssentialDisable Cortana/Siri or limit listening.
Review Installed AppsEssentialKeep apps minimal; clear caches regularly.
Manage PermissionsEssentialControl app access to location, camera, microphone, contacts.
Disable Cloud TelemetryEssentialLimit usage data sent to cloud.
Avoid Quick UnlockEssentialUse strong passwords instead of biometrics.
Power Off vs StandbyEssentialShut down encrypted devices when not in use.
Use Local AccountsOptionalAvoid linking to Microsoft/Apple accounts.
Check Sharing ServicesOptionalDisable unused network sharing.
Don't use Admin for Daily TasksOptionalUse unprivileged accounts for routine work.
Block Webcam/MicrophoneOptionalUse physical covers when not in use.
Use Privacy FilterOptionalPrevent shoulder surfing in public.
Physically Secure DeviceOptionalUse Kensington locks in public spaces.
Don't Charge Devices from PCOptionalUse power banks or wall chargers.
Randomize MAC AddressOptionalPrevent tracking across WiFi networks.
Use a FirewallOptionalMonitor and block unwanted network access.
Protect Against KeyloggersOptionalUse keystroke encryption tools.
Check Keyboard ConnectionsOptionalLook for hardware keyloggers.
Prevent Keystroke InjectionOptionalLock PC when away; use USBGuard.
Don't use Free Anti-VirusOptionalUse built-in security tools instead.
Check for RootkitsAdvancedUse tools like chkrootkit periodically.
BIOS PasswordAdvancedAdd BIOS/UEFI password for boot security.
Use Security-Focused OSAdvancedConsider Linux, QubeOS, or Tails.
Use VMsAdvancedIsolate risky activities in virtual machines.
CompartmentalizeAdvancedIsolate programs and data sources.
Disable Windows FeaturesAdvancedTurn off unnecessary background services.
Secure BootAdvancedEnable Secure Boot to prevent boot loader malware.
Secure SSHAdvancedChange default port, use SSH keys, configure firewalls.
Close Unused PortsAdvancedTurn off services on external ports.
Mandatory Access ControlAdvancedRestrict privileged access.
Use Canary TokensAdvancedDetect unauthorized file/email access.

Smart Home​

Smart home devices collect large amounts of personal data with limited user control over collection, storage, and usage.

SecurityPriorityDetails and Hints
Rename DevicesEssentialUse generic names that don't specify brand/model.
Disable Mic/CameraEssentialUse hardware switches to turn off when not in use.
Understand Data CollectionEssentialResearch data handling before purchase.
Set Privacy SettingsEssentialUse strictest privacy controls; opt-out of third-party sharing.
Don't Link to Real IdentityEssentialUse anonymous credentials.
Keep Firmware UpdatedEssentialApply security updates regularly.
Protect NetworkEssentialSecure home WiFi and network.
Be Wary of WearablesOptionalConsider extensive data collection of wearables.
Don't Connect Critical InfrastructureOptionalEvaluate risks of internet-connected thermostats, alarms.
Mitigate Smart Speaker RisksOptionalUse Mycroft or Project Alias.
Monitor NetworkOptionalUse monitoring tools for unusual activity.
Deny Internet AccessAdvancedUse firewalls to block internet for local-only devices.
Assess RisksAdvancedConsider privacy implications for all household members.

Personal Finance​

Credit card fraud is common with significant financial losses. Protect yourself from falling victim.

SecurityPriorityDetails and Hints
Fraud Alerts & MonitoringEssentialEnable through Experian, TransUnion, or Equifax.
Apply Credit FreezeEssentialPrevent unauthorized credit inquiries.
Use Virtual CardsOptionalUse services like Privacy.com or MySudo.
Use Cash LocallyOptionalAvoid financial profiling.
Use CryptocurrencyOptionalUse privacy coins like Monero for online transactions.
Store Crypto SecurelyAdvancedUse hardware wallets like Trezor or ColdCard.
Buy Crypto AnonymouslyAdvancedUse LocalBitcoins, Bisq, or Bitcoin ATMs.
Tumble/Mix CoinsAdvancedUse bitcoin mixers or CoinJoin before converting.
Use Alias DetailsAdvancedUse forwarding addresses, VOIP numbers for online shopping.
Alternate Delivery AddressAdvancedUse PO Boxes or pickup locations.

Human Aspect​

Many breaches are caused by human error. These steps reduce that risk.

SecurityPriorityDetails and Hints
Verify RecipientsEssentialVerify sender authenticity; enter URLs manually.
Don't Trust PopupsEssentialCheck URL before entering information.
Never Leave Device UnattendedEssentialUse encryption and remote erase for lost devices.
Prevent CamfectingEssentialUse webcam covers and microphone blockers.
Protect from Shoulder SurfingEssentialUse privacy screens in public.
Educate About PhishingEssentialVerify URLs, use 2FA, don't reuse passwords.
Watch for StalkerwareEssentialLook for unusual battery usage; factory reset if suspected.
Install Reputable SoftwareEssentialDownload from legitimate sources; check with Virus Total.
Store Data SecurelyEssentialEncrypt all personal data.
Obscure Personal DetailsEssentialUse opaque rectangles when sharing documents.
HTTPS Isn't EverythingEssentialVerify URLs; HTTPS doesn't guarantee legitimacy.
Use Virtual Cards OnlineOptionalProtect banking details.
Review App PermissionsOptionalManage unnecessary access regularly.
Opt-out of Public ListsOptionalRemove from databases and marketing lists.
Don't Provide PII When Opting OutOptionalAvoid additional data collection.
Opt-out of Data SharingOptionalChange default data sharing settings.
Review Social Media PrivacyOptionalUpdate settings after terms changes.
CompartmentalizeAdvancedKeep digital activities separate.
WhoIs Privacy GuardAdvancedProtect domain registration info.
Use Forwarding AddressAdvancedUse PO Box or forwarding service.
Anonymous Payment MethodsAdvancedUse cryptocurrencies.

Stay AlertEssentialBe aware of surroundings.

Physically Secure DevicesEssentialUse locks, covers, privacy screens.
Keep Devices HiddenEssentialPrevent visibility from outside.
