Completing Item 134: Conduct Solution Review Meetings

Item: 134 | Architecture Management | Risks & Blocks management | RELEASE | 1 | "The Solution Review meeting <br>is always conducted when project have signicificant technical issue/technical decission, and other Software Architects from different projects are invited to join." | The discussion was conducted in AC meeting. | | (Note: The user note "conducted in AC meeting" suggests an existing practice, possibly within an "Architecture Community" or similar group. This guide formalizes it.)

This guide explains the purpose and process of conducting Solution Review meetings, especially for significant technical decisions or issues, involving architects from outside the immediate project team.

Why Conduct Solution Reviews with External Architects? 🤔​

  • Fresh Perspectives: Architects from other projects bring different experiences, domain knowledge, and technical backgrounds, offering insights the project team might overlook.
  • Identify Blind Spots: External reviewers can more easily spot assumptions, potential risks, or missed requirements.
  • Cross-Pollination of Ideas: Shares solutions and patterns across different teams, promoting consistency and reuse within the organization.
  • Validation & Confidence: Getting positive feedback from experienced peers can increase confidence in a proposed solution. Constructive criticism helps refine it.
  • Mentoring Opportunity: Provides a learning opportunity for both the presenters and the reviewers.
  • Breaking Silos: Encourages collaboration and communication between architects across the organization.

When to Conduct a Solution Review​

Trigger these reviews for:

  • Significant New Features: Especially those with complex architecture (e.g., implementing offline sync, a new major integration).
  • Major Architectural Changes: Proposing a shift in core patterns (e.g., changing state management approach across the app).
  • Adopting New Core Technologies: Evaluating and deciding on significant new frameworks or platforms.
  • Complex Technical Problems: When the team is stuck on a difficult technical challenge and needs external input.
  • High-Risk Areas: Solutions involving critical security, performance, or scalability requirements.

Steps​

  1. Identify the Need: The project architect or tech lead identifies a topic (as listed above) that warrants broader review.
  2. Prepare the Presentation/Documentation:
    • Create a concise presentation or document (similar to a detailed ADR or the output of Item 41) explaining:
      • Problem/Context: What is the challenge or goal?
      • Proposed Solution: Describe the technical approach clearly, using diagrams.
      • Alternatives Considered: Briefly mention other options explored and why they were discarded.
      • Key Assumptions & Risks: Highlight potential challenges or uncertainties.
      • Specific Questions: List any particular areas where feedback is sought.
    • Share in Advance: Send the material to the invited reviewers at least 1-2 days before the meeting to allow them time to prepare.
  3. Schedule the Meeting:
    • Invitees:
      • Project Architect / Tech Lead (presenting).
      • Key developers from the project team involved in the solution.
      • Crucially: 2-4 experienced Software Architects from other projects or teams within the organization. Choose reviewers with relevant expertise if possible. (Leverage the "Architecture Community" or similar group if one exists).
    • Timebox: 60-90 minutes is typical.
    • Clear Goal: State the purpose (e.g., "Review proposed architecture for Offline Sync feature and gather feedback").
  4. Conduct the Meeting:
    • Presentation (15-30 min): The project architect/lead presents the problem and proposed solution concisely. Focus on the core concepts and decision points.
    • Clarifying Questions (10-15 min): Allow reviewers to ask questions to ensure they understand the proposal.
    • Feedback & Discussion (30-45 min): This is the core of the meeting. Facilitate an open discussion where external architects provide feedback, challenge assumptions, suggest alternatives, and share relevant experiences.
      • Encourage constructive criticism.
      • Keep the discussion focused on the technical solution and its trade-offs.
      • The project team should primarily listen and take notes, asking clarifying questions about the feedback received.
    • Capture Notes: Designate someone to take detailed notes of the feedback, suggestions, and any action items.
  5. Follow Up:
    • Summarize & Share: Distribute the meeting notes (especially the feedback and suggestions) to all attendees and the project team.
    • Evaluate Feedback: The project architect/lead reviews the feedback with the team.
    • Decide on Actions: Determine which suggestions to incorporate, which require further investigation (potentially a spike), and which might be disregarded (documenting the reason).
    • Update Design: Revise the solution design document/ADR based on the adopted feedback.
    • Communicate Outcome: Briefly inform the reviewers of the final decisions made based on their input, closing the loop.

Solution Reviews are a powerful tool for leveraging collective architectural expertise, improving solution quality, and mitigating risks before significant implementation effort is invested. 💡
