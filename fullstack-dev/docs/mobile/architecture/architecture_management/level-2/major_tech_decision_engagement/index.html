Completing Item 131: Engage Team in Major Technical Decisions

Item: 131 | Architecture Management | Project Plan & Architecture Roadmap | DEVELOPMENT (Sprint 1-N) | 2 | "All big technical decisions are being taken through engagement and awareness of all technical team members, architects and developers." | Don't make decisions in a vacuum. Post an ADR proposal in the dev channel for 24 hours for feedback *before* finalizing it. | |

This guide explains the importance of involving the entire technical team (architects and developers) in major technical decision-making processes.

Why Engage the Whole Team? 🤔​

  • Diverse Perspectives: Developers working directly with the code often have valuable insights into the practical implications, potential challenges, or alternative solutions that might not be immediately obvious to an architect working at a higher level.
  • Increased Buy-in & Ownership: When the team feels involved in a decision, they are more likely to understand the rationale, support the chosen path, and take ownership of its successful implementation. Decisions imposed from the top down can sometimes face passive resistance.
  • Knowledge Sharing: The discussion process itself is a valuable learning opportunity, spreading understanding of architectural concepts and trade-offs throughout the team.
  • Better Decisions: Collective intelligence and constructive debate often lead to more robust and well-considered technical solutions than decisions made in isolation.
  • Early Risk Identification: Team members might identify risks or edge cases related to a proposed solution based on their specific areas of expertise.

What Constitutes a "Big" Decision?​

Refer back to Item 19. This includes choices like core frameworks, major pattern changes, significant third-party integrations, large refactoring efforts, or decisions with major NFR impacts.

Steps for Engagement​

  1. Identify the Decision Point: When a "big" technical decision needs to be made (often identified during backlog refinement, spike investigations, or architectural planning), flag it for broader team discussion.
  2. Research & Propose (Architect/Lead): The architect or tech lead should typically perform initial research, identify viable options, and form a proposal or recommendation. This provides a starting point for discussion.
  3. Document the Proposal (e.g., Draft ADR):
    • Create a concise document (a draft Architecture Decision Record - ADR is ideal, see Item 128) outlining:
      • Problem/Context: What needs to be decided and why?
      • Options Considered: Briefly list the main alternatives.
      • Proposed Solution: Clearly state the recommended approach.
      • Rationale: Explain the reasoning, pros, and cons (technical & potentially business impact).
  4. Share Asynchronously for Feedback:
    • Post the draft ADR or proposal summary in the main technical team chat channel (e.g., #proj-mobile-tech).
    • Set a Timebox: Clearly state a deadline for feedback (e.g., "Please review this proposal for our state management approach and provide feedback/questions within the next 24-48 hours."). This prevents indefinite delays.
    • Encourage Discussion: Prompt team members to add comments directly on the document (if possible) or discuss in a dedicated chat thread.
  5. Facilitate Discussion (Synchronous if Needed):
    • If the asynchronous feedback reveals significant disagreement, complex questions, or requires deeper debate, schedule a timeboxed meeting (e.g., 30-60 minutes).
    • Meeting Goal: Focus specifically on discussing the feedback, clarifying points, debating trade-offs, and reaching a consensus or final decision.
    • Facilitation: Ensure everyone has a chance to speak, keep the discussion focused, and steer towards a decision.
  6. Make the Final Decision:
    • While consensus is ideal, the architect or tech lead ultimately needs to make the final call, considering all the input gathered.
    • Clearly communicate the final decision back to the team, explaining how their feedback influenced the outcome (even if their preferred option wasn't chosen).
  7. Finalize Documentation:
    • Update the ADR status to "Accepted."
    • Incorporate any refinements or clarifications arising from the discussion into the final ADR document.

By making major technical decisions a collaborative process, you leverage the collective expertise of your team, foster ownership, and ultimately arrive at better, more sustainable solutions. ✨
