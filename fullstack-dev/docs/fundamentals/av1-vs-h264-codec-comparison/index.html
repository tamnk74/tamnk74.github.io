AV1 vs H264 Codec: Comprehensive Comparison

Introduction​

Video codecs are essential technologies that compress and decompress digital video, making it possible to stream and store video content efficiently. This document provides an in-depth comparison between two prominent video codecs: H.264 (also known as AVC - Advanced Video Coding) and AV1 (AOMedia Video 1).

Overview​

H.264/AVC​

  • Release Year: 2003
  • Developer: ITU-T Video Coding Experts Group (VCEG) and ISO/IEC Moving Picture Experts Group (MPEG)
  • License: Proprietary (patent-encumbered, requires licensing fees)
  • Status: Industry standard, widely adopted

AV1​

  • Release Year: 2018
  • Developer: Alliance for Open Media (AOMedia) - founding members include Google, Mozilla, Netflix, Amazon, Microsoft, Intel, AMD, ARM, and Cisco
  • License: Royalty-free, open-source
  • Status: Emerging standard, rapidly gaining adoption

Technical Specifications​

Compression Efficiency​

MetricH.264AV1
Compression EfficiencyBaseline30-50% better than H.264
Bitrate ReductionReference25-35% reduction at same quality
File SizeLarger30-50% smaller at equivalent quality

AV1 achieves superior compression through:

  • Advanced prediction modes: More intra-prediction modes (56 vs H.264's 9)
  • Larger block sizes: Supports up to 128×128 superblocks
  • Improved transforms: Multiple transform types and sizes
  • Better motion compensation: Higher precision motion vectors
  • Advanced entropy coding: Context-adaptive binary arithmetic coding improvements
  • Loop filtering: Sophisticated deblocking and restoration filters

Encoding Complexity​

Encoding Speed Comparison (relative to H.264):
┌─────────────┬─────────────────────────────────┐
│ Codec       │ Encoding Speed (relative)       │
├─────────────┼─────────────────────────────────┤
│ H.264       │ 1x (baseline)                   │
│ AV1 (Fast)  │ 3-5x slower                     │
│ AV1 (Medium)│ 10-20x slower                   │
│ AV1 (Slow)  │ 50-100x slower                  │
└─────────────┴─────────────────────────────────┘

H.264 Encoding Characteristics:

  • Fast encoding speeds
  • Well-optimized encoders (x264, OpenH264)
  • Suitable for real-time encoding
  • Lower CPU/power requirements

AV1 Encoding Characteristics:

  • Significantly slower encoding
  • CPU-intensive compression
  • Requires powerful hardware for real-time encoding
  • Multiple speed presets available
  • Ongoing optimization efforts

Decoding Performance​

AspectH.264AV1
Decoding SpeedFast, highly optimized1.5-2x slower than H.264
CPU UsageLowModerate to High
Hardware SupportUniversalGrowing rapidly
Battery ImpactMinimal (with HW decode)Higher (improving with HW support)

Hardware Support​

H.264 Hardware Acceleration​

Near-Universal Support (2024-2026):

  • ✅ All modern CPUs (Intel, AMD, ARM)
  • ✅ All GPUs (NVIDIA, AMD, Intel)
  • ✅ Mobile devices (iOS, Android)
  • ✅ Smart TVs and streaming devices
  • ✅ Dedicated video encoding/decoding hardware
  • ✅ Web browsers (all major browsers)

AV1 Hardware Acceleration​

Rapidly Expanding Support:

Desktop/Laptop CPUs:

  • ✅ Intel 11th Gen (Tiger Lake) and newer - decode only
  • ✅ Intel 12th Gen (Alder Lake) and newer - encode/decode
  • ✅ AMD Ryzen 6000 series and newer - decode
  • ✅ AMD Ryzen 7000 series and newer - encode/decode
  • ✅ Apple M3 and newer - encode/decode

GPUs:

  • ✅ NVIDIA RTX 30 series (Ampere) and newer - encode/decode
  • ✅ AMD Radeon RX 6000 series and newer - decode
  • ✅ AMD Radeon RX 7000 series and newer - encode/decode
  • ✅ Intel Arc GPUs - encode/decode

Mobile Devices:

  • ✅ MediaTek Dimensity 1000 and newer
  • ✅ Qualcomm Snapdragon 8 Gen 2 and newer
  • ✅ Samsung Exynos 2200 and newer
  • ✅ Apple A17 Pro and newer, M3 and newer

Web Browsers (2026):

  • ✅ Chrome/Chromium (v90+)
  • ✅ Firefox (v67+)
  • ✅ Edge (v90+)
  • ✅ Safari (iOS 17+, macOS Sonoma+)

Quality Comparison​

Visual Quality at Same Bitrate​

AV1 Advantages:

  • Better preservation of fine details
  • Reduced blocking artifacts
  • Improved color fidelity
  • Better handling of gradients
  • Less banding in smooth areas
  • Superior performance at low bitrates

Typical Quality Metrics (at equivalent bitrate):

  • PSNR: 2-4 dB improvement
  • SSIM: 5-10% improvement
  • VMAF: 5-15 point improvement

Practical Quality Examples​

1080p Streaming:

H.264: 5 Mbps → Good quality
AV1:   3 Mbps → Equivalent or better quality (40% bitrate savings)

4K Streaming:

H.264: 25 Mbps → Good quality
AV1:   15 Mbps → Equivalent or better quality (40% bitrate savings)

Use Cases​

When to Use H.264​

✅ Best for:

  1. Real-time applications

    • Video conferencing (Zoom, Teams, Meet)
    • Live streaming with low latency requirements
    • Security cameras and surveillance systems

  2. Maximum compatibility

    • Content targeting older devices
    • Embedded systems
    • IoT devices with limited processing power

  3. Fast encoding requirements

    • Recording software
    • Game capture
    • Broadcast production

  4. Limited hardware resources

    • Mobile apps requiring efficient battery usage
    • Edge computing scenarios
    • Low-power devices

  5. Existing infrastructure

    • Systems with H.264 hardware encoders
    • Established workflows and toolchains

When to Use AV1​

✅ Best for:

  1. Video streaming platforms
    • VOD (Video on Demand) services
    • YouTube, Netflix, Vimeo
    • Significant bandwidth cost savings
  2. Storage optimization
    • Video archives
    • Cloud storage of video content
    • Mobile app video content
  3. Low-bandwidth scenarios
    • Areas with limited internet connectivity
    • Mobile data cost reduction
    • 4K/8K streaming optimization
  4. Open-source projects
    • No licensing fees
    • Patent-free distribution
  5. Future-proofing
    • New applications and platforms
    • Next-generation content delivery

Pros and Cons​

H.264 Advantages​

✅ Pros:

  • Universal hardware support
  • Fast encoding and decoding
  • Mature, battle-tested technology
  • Extensive tooling and documentation
  • Low CPU usage with hardware acceleration
  • Excellent for real-time applications
  • Predictable performance

❌ Cons:

  • Lower compression efficiency
  • Higher bandwidth requirements
  • Larger file sizes
  • Licensing fees for commercial use
  • Older technology (20+ years old)

AV1 Advantages​

✅ Pros:

  • Superior compression (30-50% better)
  • Royalty-free, open-source
  • Better visual quality at lower bitrates
  • Significant cost savings on bandwidth/storage
  • Growing ecosystem and support
  • Future-proof technology
  • Active development and optimization

❌ Cons:

  • Slower encoding (especially high-quality settings)
  • Higher CPU usage for decoding (without hardware support)
  • Limited hardware support on older devices
  • Smaller ecosystem compared to H.264
  • Encoding complexity requires powerful hardware

Implementation Considerations​

For Content Providers​

Hybrid Approach (Recommended):

  1. Encode all content in both H.264 and AV1
  2. Serve AV1 to compatible devices
  3. Fall back to H.264 for older devices
  4. Monitor hardware support trends

AV1-First Approach:

  • Suitable for platforms with modern user base
  • Significant cost savings
  • Better user experience (higher quality or lower bandwidth)
  • Requires robust fallback mechanism

Encoding Strategy​

Multi-tier encoding:

1. Fast H.264 encode (for immediate availability)
2. Background AV1 encode (for long-term serving)
3. Cache popular content in both formats
4. Analyze user agent for format selection

Browser Detection​

// JavaScript example for AV1 support detection
function supportsAV1() {
  const video = document.createElement('video');
  return video.canPlayType('video/mp4; codecs="av01.0.05M.08"') !== '';
}

// Serve appropriate format
const videoSource = supportsAV1() ? 'video-av1.mp4' : 'video-h264.mp4';

Encoding Tools and Libraries​

H.264 Encoders​

Popular Options:

  • x264: Open-source, high-quality, widely used
  • OpenH264: Cisco's open-source implementation
  • Intel Quick Sync: Hardware-accelerated
  • NVIDIA NVENC: GPU-accelerated
  • FFmpeg: Versatile, includes multiple H.264 encoders

AV1 Encoders​

Popular Options:

  • SVT-AV1 (Scalable Video Technology): Fast, production-ready, recommended
  • libaom-av1: Reference implementation, slower but highest quality
  • rav1e: Rust-based, safety and speed focused
  • FFmpeg: Includes SVT-AV1 and libaom support

Example encoding commands:

# H.264 with x264 (FFmpeg)
ffmpeg -i input.mp4 -c:v libx264 -preset medium -crf 23 -c:a aac output-h264.mp4

# AV1 with SVT-AV1 (FFmpeg)
ffmpeg -i input.mp4 -c:v libsvtav1 -preset 6 -crf 35 -c:a libopus output-av1.mp4

# Note: AV1 CRF values are typically higher (30-40) for similar quality to H.264 (18-28)

Codec Profiles and Levels​

H.264 Profiles​

  • Baseline: Basic features, mobile devices
  • Main: Improved compression, streaming
  • High: Best compression, Blu-ray, streaming
  • High 10: 10-bit color depth

AV1 Profiles​

  • Main Profile: 8-bit and 10-bit, 4:2:0 chroma subsampling
  • High Profile: 8-bit to 12-bit, 4:2:0 and 4:4:4 chroma subsampling
  • Professional Profile: Extended features for professional use

Future Outlook​

Industry Trends (2026 and Beyond)​

H.264:

  • Remains relevant for legacy support
  • Continued use in real-time applications
  • Gradual decline in new deployments
  • Hardware support will persist indefinitely

AV1:

  • Rapidly becoming the standard for streaming
  • Universal hardware support expected by 2027-2028
  • Encoder optimization continuing to improve speed
  • Growing adoption by major platforms:
    • YouTube (default for 4K+ content)
    • Netflix (expanding deployment)
    • Facebook/Meta (in rollout)
    • Amazon Prime Video (testing)

Next-Generation Codecs​

On the Horizon:

  • VVC/H.266: Successor to H.264/HEVC (patent concerns remain)
  • AV2: Next version of AV1 (in development)
  • Both promise 30-50% improvement over AV1

Conclusion​

Both H.264 and AV1 have their place in modern video workflows:

H.264 remains the go-to choice for real-time applications, maximum compatibility, and scenarios requiring fast encoding. Its maturity, universal hardware support, and predictable performance make it reliable for time-sensitive use cases.

AV1 represents the future of video streaming, offering significant improvements in compression efficiency, visual quality, and cost savings. As hardware support continues to expand and encoding speeds improve, AV1 is becoming the preferred choice for video-on-demand services and bandwidth-conscious applications.

Recommendation: For most modern applications, implement a hybrid approach that serves AV1 to capable devices while maintaining H.264 compatibility for older hardware. This strategy maximizes quality and cost savings while ensuring universal accessibility.

As of 2026, the transition from H.264 to AV1 is well underway, and organizations should be actively planning their migration strategy to take advantage of AV1's superior efficiency while maintaining backward compatibility.
