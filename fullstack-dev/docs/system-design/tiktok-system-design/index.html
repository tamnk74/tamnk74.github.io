TikTok System Design: Short-Form Video Platform at Global Scale

Overview​

TikTok is a large-scale short-form video platform that combines mobile video creation, low-latency media delivery, and a highly personalized recommendation system. The core product challenge is not only storing and serving massive volumes of video, but deciding what to show next for each user within a few hundred milliseconds.

This document outlines a practical system design for a TikTok-like platform, with emphasis on feed ranking, media processing, global delivery, safety, and operational resilience.

Problem Statement​

Design a short-form video platform that allows users to:

  • Upload and publish videos with captions, hashtags, sounds, and effects
  • Watch an endless personalized feed with very low startup latency
  • Interact with content through likes, comments, shares, follows, saves, and watch-time signals
  • Discover content via search, hashtag pages, creator profiles, and trends
  • Receive safe, policy-compliant recommendations at global scale

The most important product surface is the For You feed, where videos are ranked from a combination of user interactions, video metadata, and contextual signals. The design must optimize for both engagement and diversity while preventing spam, policy violations, and repeated content.

Forces​

Functional Requirements​

  • Video Upload: Users can upload short videos, add music, captions, tags, and publish
  • Video Processing: Transcode videos into multiple formats and bitrates for mobile playback
  • Feed Personalization: Generate a unique ranked feed per user session
  • Engagement: Likes, comments, shares, watch completion, rewatches, follows
  • Search & Discovery: Hashtags, sounds, creators, trending topics, keyword search
  • Social Graph: Follow/unfollow creators and surface following feeds
  • Moderation & Safety: Detect spam, abuse, unsafe content, policy violations
  • Notifications: Push events for likes, comments, follows, mentions, and live activity
  • Analytics: Creator insights, trend analysis, and online experimentation

Non-Functional Requirements​

Scale Assumptions​

  • Monthly active users: 1B+
  • Daily active users: 250M+
  • Videos uploaded per day: 50M+
  • Average video size after compression: 8-20 MB source asset
  • Average session length: 30-60 minutes
  • Feed requests: 500K+ RPS globally at peak
  • Engagement events: Tens of millions per second during peak traffic

Core Design: Recommendation System​

Why Recommendation Is the Center of the Architecture​

TikTok’s primary differentiator is not social-following retrieval; it is interest-based ranking. The system should quickly infer what a user is likely to watch to completion, rewatch, share, or follow, while also preserving novelty and content diversity.

Public TikTok guidance indicates the For You feed uses a combination of:

  • User interactions: likes, shares, follows, comments, watch completion, replays, skips, “Not Interested”
  • Video information: captions, hashtags, sounds, topics, language, effects
  • Contextual signals: device type, language, country, session context

These signals should not flow through one monolithic model. A layered ranking pipeline is simpler to scale and easier to tune.

Multi-Stage Ranking Pipeline​

Candidate Sources​

  • Following graph: creators the user follows
  • Collaborative filtering: users with similar behavior
  • Embedding similarity: videos semantically close to recently watched content
  • Trending pools: fast-rising videos in the user’s language/region
  • Exploration buckets: intentionally diverse or fresh content for novelty

Eligibility and Re-ranking Rules​

Before ranking or final delivery, remove content that is:

  • Under moderation review
  • Reported or policy restricted
  • Duplicate or already over-served in recent sessions
  • Repetitive by creator, sound, or hashtag beyond fatigue thresholds
  • Incompatible with age, country, or safety settings

The final re-ranker should explicitly optimize for:

  • Diversity: avoid near-identical sequences
  • Freshness: mix recent uploads with evergreen content
  • Exploration: occasionally test new categories
  • Session health: reduce monotony and unsafe rabbit holes

Implementation Guide for Recommendation System​

Stage 1: Candidate Retrieval (Fast, Broad)​

Goal: Retrieve ~200-500 candidate videos in < 50ms from multiple sources. Quantity over quality.

Implementation approach:

# Pseudocode: retrieve candidates from multiple sources in parallel
async def retrieve_candidates(user_id, limit=500):
    tasks = [
        fetch_following_feed(user_id, limit=150),
        fetch_collaborative_candidates(user_id, limit=150),
        fetch_embedding_similar(user_id, limit=100),
        fetch_trending_pool(user_id, limit=100),
    ]
    results = await asyncio.gather(*tasks, return_exceptions=True)
    return deduplicate_and_merge(results, limit)

# Following graph: fast lookup in graph store
def fetch_following_feed(user_id, limit):
    follows = graph_store.get_followers(user_id, limit=100)
    video_ids = video_store.recent_by_creators(
        follows,
        hours_back=7,
        limit=limit
    )
    return video_ids

# Collaborative filtering: offline trained neighbors
def fetch_collaborative_candidates(user_id, limit):
    similar_users = embedding_index.search(
        user_embedding(user_id),
        k=500,
        index_name="user_embeddings"
    )
    return video_store.top_videos_by_users(similar_users, limit=limit)

# Dense retrieval: vector search for content similarity
def fetch_embedding_similar(user_id, limit):
    watched_videos = engagement_store.recent_watches(user_id, limit=20)
    avg_embedding = mean([video_embedding(v) for v in watched_videos])
    
    candidates = vector_db.search(
        query_vector=avg_embedding,
        index_name="video_content_embeddings",
        limit=limit,
        exclude=watched_videos  # no repeats
    )
    return candidates

Key design choices:

  • Parallel retrieval: no sequential IO; use async/await or thread pools
  • Source weighting: tag each candidate by source to measure contribution to engagement
  • Deduplication: use a set to remove videos appearing in multiple sources
  • Early filtering: remove blocked, moderated, or already-seen content immediately after retrieval

Stage 2: Feature Engineering​

Online features (low-latency, read-optimized):

  • User: recent watch history embeddings, follow count, language, country, device
  • Video: view/like/share counts, creator reputation, age, language, sound popularity
  • Context: time-of-day, session length, user's session sequence

Offline features (batch-computed every few hours):

  • User-video collaborative filtering score
  • User-category affinity (derived weekly from historical watch behavior)
  • Video trending score in user's region

Implementation:

# Feature retrieval in online path
class FeatureStore:
    def __init__(self, redis_online, warehouse_offline):
        self.online = redis_online  # low-latency
        self.offline = warehouse_offline  # batch features
    
    async def get_user_features(self, user_id):
        # Online: recent watch embeddings, basic profile
        recent_watches = await self.online.get(f"user:{user_id}:watches", limit=20)
        user_embedding = await self.online.get(f"user:{user_id}:embedding")
        
        # Offline: affinity scores (refreshed daily)
        affinities = await self.offline.get(f"user_affinities:{user_id}")
        
        return {
            "embedding": user_embedding,
            "recent_watches": recent_watches,
            "affinities": affinities,
        }
    
    async def get_video_features(self, video_ids):
        # Batch fetch from online store
        features = await self.online.mget([
            f"video:{vid}:metadata" for vid in video_ids
        ])
        return features

Stage 3: First-Stage Ranker (Lightweight, ~100ms for 500 videos)​

A fast linear or shallow model that filters candidates to top-K (50-100).

Model type: Logistic regression or 2-layer neural net.

Optimization: Use ONNX Runtime or TensorFlow Lite for inference speed.

# First-stage model: logistic regression on handcrafted features
class FirstStageRanker:
    def __init__(self, model_path):
        self.session = onnxruntime.InferenceSession(model_path)
    
    def score_candidates(self, user_features, video_features_list):
        # Stack features into batch
        X = np.array([
            self._combine_features(user_features, video_feat)
            for video_feat in video_features_list
        ])
        
        scores = self.session.run(None, {"input": X.astype(np.float32)})[0]
        return scores.flatten()
    
    def _combine_features(self, user_feat, video_feat):
        return np.concatenate([
            user_feat["embedding"],
            video_feat["view_count_log"],
            video_feat["creator_reputation"],
            # ... other features
        ])

Stage 4: Second-Stage Ranker (Heavy, 200-300ms for 50-100 videos)​

Deep learning model with richer feature interactions; high precision ranking.

Model architecture: multi-tower (user tower + video tower) or transformer-based.

Key implementation details:

  • Hosted on low-latency ML serving platform (TFServing, KServe, Seldon, or Triton)
  • Batch inference: queue incoming ranking requests for 10-50ms Windows for better GPU utilization
  • Model versioning: maintain shadow serving for A/B tests
# Second-stage model: deep ranking tower
class SecondStageRanker:
    def __init__(self, serving_url):
        self.client = grpc_client.StubFor(serving_url)
    
    async def rank_videos(self, user_id, video_ids):
        # Fetch rich features
        user_emb = await user_embedding_store.get(user_id)
        video_embs = await video_embedding_store.mget(video_ids)
        
        # Batch request to ML server
        request = RankingRequest(
            user_embedding=user_emb,
            video_embeddings=video_embs,
            context=self._build_context()
        )
        
        response = await self.client.Rank(request)
        return list(zip(video_ids, response.scores))
    
    def _build_context(self):
        return Context(
            hour_of_day=datetime.now().hour,
            device_type="mobile",
            session_length_sec=elapsed_seconds,
        )

Stage 5: Diversity and Re-ranking​

After heavy ranking, apply business rules to ensure quality and diversity.

async def apply_diversity_and_policy(ranked_videos, user_id):
    result = []
    seen_creators = set()
    seen_sounds = set()
    max_per_creator = 2  # Avoid repetition
    max_per_sound = 1
    
    for video_id, score in ranked_videos:
        video_meta = await video_store.get(video_id)
        
        # Creator repetition check
        if video_meta.creator_id in seen_creators:
            if seen_creators[video_meta.creator_id] >= max_per_creator:
                continue
        
        # Sound repetition check
        if video_meta.sound_id in seen_sounds:
            if seen_sounds[video_meta.sound_id] >= max_per_sound:
                continue
        
        # Safety gate: check recommendation eligibility
        if not await safety_service.is_recommendable(video_id):
            continue
        
        # Session fatigue: check if user has watched too much of this category
        if await engagement_store.session_category_count(user_id, video_meta.category) > 3:
            # Inject exploration: skip to next video
            continue
        
        result.append(video_id)
        seen_creators[video_meta.creator_id] = \
            seen_creators.get(video_meta.creator_id, 0) + 1
        seen_sounds[video_meta.sound_id] = \
            seen_sounds.get(video_meta.sound_id, 0) + 1
        
        if len(result) >= 20:  # Return 20 videos per feed page
            break
    
    return result

Stage 6: Online Serving with Caching​

class FeedService:
    def __init__(self, cache, candidate_svc, ranker_1, ranker_2):
        self.cache = cache
        self.candidate_svc = candidate_svc
        self.ranker_1 = ranker_1
        self.ranker_2 = ranker_2
    
    async def get_feed(self, user_id, session_id, pagination_token=None):
        cache_key = f"feed:{user_id}:{session_id}"
        
        # Check for cached batch
        cached = await self.cache.get(cache_key)
        if cached and not pagination_token:
            return cached
        
        # Retrieve candidates
        candidates = await self.candidate_svc.retrieve(user_id, limit=500)
        
        # Fetch features
        user_feat = await feature_store.get_user_features(user_id)
        video_feats = await feature_store.get_video_features(candidates)
        
        # First stage: filter to top 100
        first_stage_scores = self.ranker_1.score_candidates(user_feat, video_feats)
        top_100 = sorted(
            zip(candidates, first_stage_scores),
            key=lambda x: x[1],
            reverse=True
        )[:100]
        
        # Second stage: rank top 50
        top_50 = await self.ranker_2.rank_videos(user_id, [v for v, _ in top_100])
        top_50_sorted = sorted(top_50, key=lambda x: x[1], reverse=True)
        
        # Apply diversity and policy
        final_feed = await apply_diversity_and_policy(top_50_sorted, user_id)
        
        # Cache for 60 seconds (user might scroll quickly)
        await self.cache.set(cache_key, final_feed, ttl=60)
        
        return final_feed

Experimentation Framework​

To improve the ranker, run continuous A/B tests:

class ExperimentationFramework:
    async def run_feed_with_experiment(self, user_id, experiment_config):
        """
        experiment_config = {
            "treatment": "new_ranking_v2",
            "control": "current_ranking",
        }
        """
        
        # Bucketing: deterministic assignment based on user_id hash
        bucket = hash(user_id) % 100
        
        if bucket < 50:
            variant = "control"
            ranker = self.ranker_v1
        else:
            variant = "treatment"
            ranker = self.ranker_v2  # new model
        
        # Get candidates and rank with variant ranker
        candidates = await self.candidate_svc.retrieve(user_id)
        ranked = await ranker.rank_videos(user_id, candidates)
        
        # Log for analysis
        await analytics.log_experiment_event(
            user_id=user_id,
            variant=variant,
            videos_shown=ranked,
            timestamp=now(),
        )
        
        return ranked

Media Processing and Delivery​

Media Pipeline​

TikTok-like apps are video systems before they are recommendation systems. Playback quality and startup time directly affect retention, so the media path must be optimized aggressively.

Delivery Choices​

  • Adaptive bitrate streaming with HLS or DASH
  • Pre-signed playback manifests to enforce geo/policy constraints
  • Aggressive edge caching for popular videos
  • Thumbnail and first-segment prefetching for faster swipe transitions
  • Regional origin selection to minimize startup latency

Capacity Planning​

Assume:

  • 50M uploads/day
  • 15 MB average source asset
  • 3 renditions/video on average after transcode

Estimated daily source ingest:

50,000,000×15 MB=750,000,000 MB≈750 TB/day50,000,000 \times 15\text{ MB} = 750,000,000\text{ MB} \approx 750\text{ TB/day}50,000,000×15 MB=750,000,000 MB≈750 TB/day

With multiple renditions and replication, practical media storage and egress easily move into multi-petabyte ranges.

Safety, Moderation, and Integrity​

Why Safety Must Be in the Hot Path​

For a recommendation-led product, unsafe content can spread much faster than in follower-only networks. Moderation cannot be an afterthought or an offline reporting system. Safety gates must affect recommendation eligibility early.

Moderation Pipeline​

Integrity Controls​

  • Device fingerprinting and rate limiting for bot farms
  • Engagement fraud detection on likes, follows, and comments
  • Creator reputation scores to reduce repeated abuse
  • Policy labels affecting whether a video is playable, discoverable, or recommendable
  • Human review tools for appeals and edge cases

Search and Discovery​

TikTok discovery is broader than keyword search. The system should support:

  • Hashtag pages
  • Sound pages
  • Creator profiles
  • Topic and trend exploration
  • Basic semantic similarity for content/video search

The architecture should separate:

  • Search index for text, creators, hashtags, and metadata
  • Recommendation retrieval for personalized feed ranking
  • Trend jobs for rapidly rising content by region/language/category

Infrastructure and Regional Architecture​

Regional Strategy​

  • Active-active serving for feed and playback APIs
  • Geo-local playback with regional origins and CDN shielding
  • Regional data controls for privacy and compliance requirements
  • Asynchronous replication for analytics and non-critical aggregates
  • Strong consistency only where needed: account changes, follow/block actions, policy states

Failure Modes​

Failure ModeUser ImpactMitigation
CDN edge degradationSlower playback startupMulti-CDN failover, regional origin fallback
Transcode backlog spikeNewly uploaded videos delayedPriority queues, autoscaled worker pools, degraded thumbnail-first availability
Feature store outageLower ranking qualityFallback to cached features and heuristic ranking
Ranking service overloadFeed latency increasesPrecomputed candidate batches, per-user cache, lightweight fallback ranker
Kafka lag in analytics pipelineSlower model updatesDecouple serving from analytics; backpressure monitoring
Safety service timeoutRecommendation riskFail closed for new content eligibility; retry asynchronously
Hot creator traffic burstCache stampede, origin overloadRequest coalescing, hot-key sharding, stronger CDN caching

Trade-offs​

DecisionBenefitCost
Multi-stage ranking instead of one heavy modelLower latency and better explainabilityMore pipeline complexity
Event-driven ingestion and engagementDecouples hot paths and analyticsRequires strong observability and idempotency
NoSQL for video metadataHigh write throughput and scaleWeaker ad hoc query support than SQL

Safety gating before broad recommendationReduces harmful spreadCan delay distribution of some legitimate content

When to Use This Design​

This design fits products that need:

  • Personalized short-form video feeds
  • Massive media upload and playback scale
  • Heavy ML-driven ranking with low latency
  • Strong moderation controls in the recommendation path
  • Global mobile-first delivery with high traffic volatility

It is likely too complex for:

  • Small creator platforms with low upload volume
  • Products where follower-only retrieval is enough
  • Teams without streaming data, ML, and media infrastructure capabilities
